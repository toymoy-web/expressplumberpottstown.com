When A Leak Isn't Where You'd Expect

A lot of the leak calls we get start the same way: the water bill went up and nobody can find where it's coming from, or there's a warm or damp spot on a floor with no visible pipe nearby. In Pottstown's older homes, and the similarly aged housing stock in Phoenixville, a leak inside a wall or under a slab often isn't detectable by sight until real damage has already happened.

A leak doesn't have to be dramatic to be expensive. A pinhole leak losing even a slow, steady drip around the clock adds up to real gallons over a billing cycle, often before anyone notices a puddle anywhere in the house.

How We Find It

Acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure, even through a slab or wall. Where that's inconclusive, we use moisture meters and, when needed, thermal imaging to narrow down a hidden leak's location before opening anything up. Finding the exact spot first is what keeps the repair itself small.

A meter test, shutting off every fixture in the house and watching whether the meter still moves, is usually the first and cheapest step, and it tells us immediately whether the leak is inside the house or between the meter and the street, the same distinction that separates this service from a full water line repair.

Most residential water meters have a small leak indicator dial that spins even at a trickle, so a technician can often confirm an active leak in under a minute once every fixture in the house is genuinely off, including ice makers and irrigation systems people tend to forget about.

Common Leak Sources We Find

Pinhole leaks in aging copper, a slab leak under a concrete foundation, a failing water heater connection, and a leaking irrigation or outdoor line are the most common sources. Each points to a different fix, which is why we confirm the source before quoting a repair rather than guessing from the symptom alone.

Leak repairs on any supply line fall under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the model code Pottstown Borough generally follows, and a repair on an older line sometimes surfaces a code issue with the surrounding installation that we'll flag honestly rather than just patch around.

An outdoor irrigation leak is easy to overlook in winter when the system is shut down, only to surface as a mystery bill increase the following spring once it's turned back on.

What To Do While You Wait

Check your water meter

Shut off every fixture and watch the meter. If it's still moving, you have an active leak.

Note the warm or damp spot

A warm spot often means a hot water line leak; tell us which it is.

Don't cut into drywall yourself

Opening a wall before we've located the source can miss the actual leak entirely.

Shut off the main if it's severe

An active, visible leak that's actively flooding warrants a full water shutoff until we arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you find a leak without cutting into walls?

Acoustic listening equipment and moisture meters usually locate a hidden leak precisely enough that we only open the wall or slab at the actual repair point.

My water bill went up but I don't see any water. Could it still be a leak?

Yes. A slab leak or an underground line leak can run for weeks before it's visible anywhere in the house.

Do you offer leak detection in Limerick and the other towns you serve?

Yes, leak detection is available throughout our full service area.

Can a hidden leak affect my water pressure?

Yes, a significant leak upstream of a fixture can reduce pressure there, which is sometimes the first sign someone notices before checking the water bill.

How much does leak detection typically cost?

It depends on the method needed. A simple meter test is inexpensive; thermal imaging or extended acoustic work costs more, and we'll tell you which applies before starting.
