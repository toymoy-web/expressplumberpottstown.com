Which Pipes Freeze First In Pottstown Homes

Pipes running through an uninsulated crawlspace, an exterior wall, or an unheated basement corner are the most common to freeze during a hard cold snap. Older homes with original, minimal insulation around these runs are more exposed than newer construction built to current energy codes.

A kitchen sink on an exterior wall is a classic trouble spot, since the cabinet below often blocks warm room air from reaching the supply lines behind it, effectively creating its own unheated micro-space inside an otherwise heated house.

Safe Thawing, Not Guesswork

We use controlled heat sources designed for pipe thawing, never an open flame, which can scorch a pipe or ignite nearby framing. We also check the full run for other cold spots once one section is found frozen, since a single freeze point is often not the only vulnerable section on that line.

A hair dryer or heat lamp aimed at an exposed section can work for a minor freeze caught early, but a line that's fully blocked or in a hard-to-reach spot usually needs professional equipment to thaw safely without risking a scorched pipe or an electrical hazard from water near a plugged-in heat source.

If It's Already Burst

A frozen pipe that has already split needs the water shut off first, then a section repair or replacement once thawed enough to assess the damage. See our burst pipe repair page for what to do immediately if you have active water flowing.

Pipe insulation and support requirements for any repair follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Pottstown Borough generally applies, and we'll bring an exposed run up to that standard as part of the repair rather than simply replacing the damaged section in isolation.

Why This Happens More In Certain Parts Of A House

Pipes near an exterior wall, in an unheated crawlspace, or running through a garage lose heat faster than anything centrally located, and a hard freeze finds that weak point first. An older Pottstown home built before current insulation standards existed is more likely to have supply lines running through one of these vulnerable spots without anyone realizing it until the cold snap hits.

A single-digit overnight low sustained for more than a few hours is usually what it takes to freeze an exposed line, which is why the risk here tracks specific cold snaps rather than the season as a whole. Knowing which nights actually warrant leaving a faucet dripping matters more than dripping continuously all winter.

Preventing The Next Freeze

Let faucets drip during a hard freeze

A small trickle of water keeps pressure from building in a pipe that's starting to freeze.

Open cabinet doors under sinks

Letting warm room air reach supply lines on exterior walls helps prevent freezing.

Insulate exposed pipes

Crawlspace and basement pipes on exterior walls benefit most from added insulation.

Know your main shutoff

If a pipe does freeze and split, you'll want to act immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if a pipe is frozen versus just not working?

No water from one or more fixtures during a cold snap, especially ones on an exterior wall, is the classic sign of a frozen line.

Is it safe to thaw a pipe myself with a hair dryer?

It can be, applied gently and only if the pipe hasn't already split. We recommend calling if you're unsure, since a split pipe under pressure can cause serious damage fast.

Can you help prevent this next winter?

Yes, we can insulate exposed lines and identify vulnerable sections before the next cold snap.

Does homeowners insurance cover frozen pipe damage?

Often yes, for the sudden damage from a burst. Check your specific policy, since some require proof reasonable precautions like heating were maintained.
