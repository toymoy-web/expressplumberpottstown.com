Why Pipes Burst In Pottstown Homes

Freeze-thaw cycles in an uninsulated wall or crawlspace are the most common cause locally, followed by simple age in original galvanized supply lines that have been under pressure for 60 or more years. A burst almost always happens at the weakest point in the system, which is usually the oldest section, not necessarily the coldest one.

Water expands by roughly 9 percent as it freezes, and that expansion is what actually splits the pipe, not the cold itself. The break usually shows up downstream of the ice blockage once the water thaws and pressure returns, which is why the visible leak point isn't always exactly where the freeze happened.

What We Do First

Stopping the water is the immediate priority, at the fixture shutoff if there is one, or the main shutoff if the burst is upstream of any isolation valve. Once the water is off, we locate the actual break, assess how much of the surrounding pipe is compromised, and repair or replace that section.

We also check for water that's already migrated beyond the immediate area, since a burst behind a wall or above a ceiling often travels along framing before it becomes visible, meaning the repair area and the water damage area aren't always the same footprint.

Repair Vs. Broader Replacement

A single burst in an otherwise sound pipe usually just needs a section repair. A burst in a line that's already showing corrosion elsewhere is a sign the whole run may be near the end of its life, and we'll tell you honestly if a broader repipe makes more sense than repeated emergency repairs on the same aging line.

Any repair or replacement work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Pottstown Borough generally applies, which matters most when the burst reveals that the original installation, common in homes this age, no longer meets current standards for pipe support or material.

We also check the rest of the exposed run while we're already in that section, since a burst in one spot on an aging galvanized line is often an early warning for the rest of it, not an isolated event.

Right Now, Before We Arrive

Shut off the main water valve

This is the single most important step. Know where it is before you need it.

Shut off power near standing water

If water is near an outlet or panel, shut off power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to reach.

Open a faucet to relieve pressure

Opening the nearest faucet after shutting off the main helps drain remaining pressure from the line.

Move belongings away from the water

Protect what you can before water spreads further.
