An Ironworking Town With Its Own Water System

Boyertown became a borough in 1866, built on iron mining and the Colebrookdale Furnace, among Pennsylvania's earliest pig-iron furnaces. That industrial past left the town running its own municipal water and wastewater utility, drawing from a surface reservoir fed by the Popodickon and Trout Run Dams, separate from Pottstown's own Schuylkill-based supply. The wastewater plant discharges into Swamp Creek, not the Schuylkill River, since Boyertown sits on the Manatawny Creek watershed instead.

That distinction matters for a service call. A home on Boyertown Borough water deals with a regulated, EPA and DEP-tested municipal supply, treated and monitored under standards set by Pennsylvania's Department of Environmental Protection. A home just outside the borough line, in Colebrookdale or Washington Township, is more likely on a private well drawing from local bedrock aquifers that commonly test 10 to 20 grains per gallon of hardness, solidly in the "very hard" category.

Plumbing installation and repair work in Boyertown, whether inside the borough or in the surrounding townships, still falls under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that most Pennsylvania municipalities apply, though water treatment recommendations differ sharply depending on which water source a given address actually uses.

What We See Most In Boyertown

On private wells outside the borough proper, water softener and filtration calls are more common here than in any of our other service towns, given how consistently hard the groundwater tests. Inside the borough on municipal water, the calls look more like Pottstown's own mix, water heater sediment buildup and drain line age in the borough's older ironworking-era housing near downtown and Boyertown Community Park.

Homes built during Boyertown's 19th-century industrial growth, many within easy walking distance of the historic downtown along Route 100, tend to share the same original supply-line material issues we see in Pottstown's own mid-century housing, just on a genuinely different water chemistry. A newer home further out toward Colebrookdale Township is more likely on a private well and more likely to need a first-time water test rather than a repair on existing softening equipment already installed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Boyertown in Montgomery County like Pottstown?

No, Boyertown itself is in Berks County, though it borders Douglass Township in Montgomery County. We're familiar with the boundary since it affects which municipal rules apply.

Is Boyertown's well water really that hard?

Often yes. Private wells in the surrounding townships commonly test 10 to 20 grains per gallon, which is considered very hard and worth addressing with a softener.

How far is Boyertown from your Pottstown base?

About 10 to 12 miles via Routes 100 and 422, well within our regular same-day service radius for both routine visits and emergency calls.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Boyertown?

Yes, Boyertown is served entirely by one ZIP code, 19512, and our coverage reaches every part of it, from the downtown ironworking-era core out to the surrounding township edges.

Is emergency service available in Boyertown?
