Why Drains Clog Faster In Older Pottstown Homes

A lot of Pottstown's housing stock dates to the mid-20th century, and the original drain lines in those homes have had 60 to 70 years to accumulate grease, mineral scale, and root intrusion at the joints. That head start means a clog here often has more buildup behind it than the same symptom would in a newer subdivision.

Pottstown's moderately hard water plays a role here too, the same mineral content that shortens a water heater's working life also narrows drain diameter over decades as scale builds up along the pipe wall. A line that's lost a third of its usable diameter to scale clogs far more easily with normal daily use than a clean one does.

Signs You Need A Drain Cleared

Water draining slower than it used to, gurgling from a drain when another fixture runs, a bad smell coming up from a floor drain, and a full backup in a sink or tub are the most common calls we get. A backup that shows up in multiple fixtures at once, rather than just one sink or tub, usually points to a clog further down the line rather than in that one fixture.

How We Clear It

A cable auger handles most single-fixture clogs, snaking through the trap and line to break up or pull out the blockage. For a line with years of grease buildup or root intrusion, hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full diameter of the pipe clean, rather than just punching a hole through the clog the way a cable does.

For a drain that clogs repeatedly despite cabling, a camera inspection shows us exactly what's happening inside the line, whether that's root intrusion at a joint, a bellied section holding standing water, or a partial collapse that cabling alone will never permanently fix. We recommend a camera inspection when a repeat clog pattern suggests the real problem is structural, not just buildup.

A Note On Prevention

A drain that's cleared once and never maintained tends to clog again on roughly the same timeline, especially in a kitchen line or a bathroom drain that sees daily grease and hair. Running hot water after each use and avoiding pouring grease down a kitchen sink extends the time between real clogs, though it won't undo decades of existing buildup in an older line. A yearly preventive cabling on a known problem drain is often cheaper than an emergency call.

Before We Arrive

Stop using chemical drain cleaners

Repeated chemical cleaner use can damage older pipes and makes it harder for us to safely clear the line by hand.

Don't keep plunging a backed-up toilet

If a toilet is backing up with sewage rather than just clogged, plunging can push the problem further down the line.

Note which fixtures are affected

Tell us if it's just one drain or several, since that changes where the clog likely is.

Shut off the water to an overflowing fixture

If a toilet or sink is actively overflowing, shut off its supply valve while you wait.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you offer emergency drain cleaning at night?

Yes, our line is answered 24 hours a day for backups and overflow situations.

Is hydro jetting always necessary?

No. A standard cable auger clears most single clogs. We only recommend hydro jetting when there's significant grease buildup, root intrusion, or a drain that clogs repeatedly.

Do you clear drains in Limerick and Collegeville too?

Yes, drain cleaning is available throughout our Montgomery County service area, including Limerick and Collegeville.

Can chemical drain cleaner damage my pipes?

Repeated use can, especially in older Pottstown homes with original galvanized or cast iron lines. We recommend calling for a mechanical clearing instead of repeat chemical treatments.
