A Small Riverfront Borough With Real Flood Exposure

Royersford was incorporated in 1879 out of Limerick Township's southeastern corner, taking its name from a Schuylkill River crossing once owned by the Royer family. At around 4,700 residents, it's the smallest town in our service area, and its riverfront location means Schuylkill flooding is a named risk in the borough's own planning documents, not just a Pottstown concern upstream.

The borough's older housing, some dating to 1817, and its industrial-era iron and manufacturing history left a mix of aging drain infrastructure that, combined with the river's high water table, makes basement seepage and sump pump strain a real and recurring issue here.

What We See Most In Royersford

Sump pump and basement waterproofing calls are more common here relative to our other service towns, given the direct river exposure. Drain line age in the borough's older housing stock is the second most common issue we see.

The Spring-Ford Area Historical Society is headquartered in the William Lewin Plantation House, built in 1861, in a part of the borough that reflects Royersford's shift from a farming hub to an iron and manufacturing town in the late 1800s. Homes from that manufacturing-era expansion make up much of Royersford's older housing stock, and their drain lines have had over a century to accumulate the buildup that leads to the clogs we get called out for most often.

Waterproofing To A Real Standard, Not A Guess

Foundation drainage work in Royersford follows the same footing-drainage principle behind the International Residential Code (IRC), the model code most Pennsylvania municipalities use. Given the borough's documented flood exposure, that standard matters more here than in a town further from the river, and we'll document the specific repair scope and cost before scheduling any work so there's no surprise once the job is done.

At around 4,700 residents, Royersford is small enough that we're often already familiar with a given street's typical foundation type and drainage history before we arrive, since the same riverfront blocks tend to call with the same underlying issue year after year. That local familiarity is part of why a Royersford sump pump call gets treated with the same urgency as an active leak, even before we know exactly how bad it is.

What To Do If Your Basement Is Taking On Water

Don't enter standing water near outlets

If electrical outlets or a panel are near or under water, stay out and shut off power at the main if it's safe to reach.

Check your sump pump first

A tripped breaker or a stuck float switch is a quick fix that can stop the problem before it gets worse.

Move belongings off the floor

Get anything on the basement floor up onto shelving before it's affected.

Call for active flooding, not just a damp floor

Active water intrusion needs a faster response than a scheduled waterproofing visit.
