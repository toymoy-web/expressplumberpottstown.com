If You Smell Gas, This Isn't A Scheduled Call

A sulfur or rotten-egg smell near an appliance or along a gas line means you should leave the gas on only long enough to shut off the appliance's own valve if it's safe to reach, then evacuate and call your gas utility's emergency line before calling us. We handle the repair once the utility has confirmed it's safe to be in the home.

Natural gas itself is odorless; the sulfur smell is an additive called mercaptan, deliberately mixed in specifically so leaks are detectable by smell. If you can smell it at all, that's the system working as designed, not a sign the leak is unusually severe.

Common Gas Line Work We Handle

Extending a line to a new gas range, dryer, or outdoor fire feature; repairing a line damaged during other work like landscaping or a renovation; and upsizing a line to support a new gas tankless water heater's higher demand are the most common jobs. Each requires sizing the line correctly for the appliance's BTU demand, not just connecting to the nearest existing pipe.

A line that was correctly sized for an older, lower-demand appliance, common throughout Collegeville's wide range of housing ages, often can't support a newer, higher-efficiency unit without an upgrade, which is why we calculate actual BTU load before connecting any new appliance rather than assume the existing line has enough capacity.

Code Compliance And Inspection

Gas line work in Pottstown Borough is governed by the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC), which works alongside the International Plumbing Code (IPC) the borough generally applies to other plumbing work. Any new gas line or appliance connection typically requires a permit and inspection, and we handle both as part of the job rather than skip a step that exists for real safety reasons.

A pressure test on the completed line, checking for any leak before the appliance is connected and put into service, is a required part of the inspection process, not an optional extra step we can skip to save time.

Older homes sometimes have a mix of black iron and copper gas piping installed at different renovation points over the decades, and connecting a new line correctly to that existing mix requires understanding what's actually there, not assuming a single uniform material throughout.

We identify the existing material before connecting anything new to it, since mixing dissimilar metals incorrectly can create a corrosion problem at the joint that wasn't there in either original material alone.

What To Do If You Smell Gas

Don't operate any switches or flames

Avoid light switches, appliances, and open flames, any spark can ignite a leak.

Leave the area immediately

Get everyone out of the house or building before doing anything else.

Call the gas utility's emergency line first

They can remotely shut off service and confirm when it's safe to re-enter.

Don't re-enter until cleared

Wait for utility confirmation before going back in, even if the smell seems to have faded.

Frequently Asked Questions

I smell gas. What do I do right now?

Leave the area, don't operate any switches or flames, and call your gas utility's emergency line first, then us once it's confirmed safe.

Can you add a gas line for an outdoor fire pit or grill?

Yes, we size and run lines for outdoor gas features, all to code and inspected.

Do I need a bigger gas line for a tankless water heater?

Often yes. Gas tankless units have higher BTU demand than a tank unit, and we check your existing line before recommending a conversion.

How long does a typical gas line installation take?

A straightforward extension to a new appliance is often completed in a few hours; permit and inspection timing can add days to the overall project.

Can you convert an appliance from electric to gas?

Yes, this typically requires running a new gas line to the appliance location, which we size and install to code.
