Renting Near Ursinus College? What To Know About Older Collegeville Plumbing

By the Pottstown Express Plumbing team · Plumbing Tips

Have Questions?

Housing near Ursinus College in Collegeville has a real, distinct pattern: a lot of it started life as single-family housing before being converted into student rentals over the decades, and that conversion history matters more for plumbing than most tenants or landlords realize.

Why Near-Campus Housing Is Different

Collegeville's housing stock overall ranges from 1850s original construction to newer builds, one of the widest age ranges of any town in our service area. Properties near campus specifically have an additional layer of history on top of that age range: many were converted from single-family homes into multi-unit or higher-occupancy student rentals at some point, sometimes decades ago, sometimes more recently.

A conversion like this means plumbing originally designed for one household's typical water use is now serving considerably more people, more showers, more laundry, more overall demand, often without the underlying supply lines, drain capacity, or water heater ever being upgraded to match. That mismatch is the root of a lot of the specific problems we see in this kind of property.

Water Pressure Problems That Aren't Really About Pressure

A common complaint in converted rental properties is inconsistent water pressure, particularly when multiple showers or fixtures run at once, something that happens more often in a higher-occupancy rental than it would in the same house under original single-family use. This often isn't actually a pressure problem from the utility; it's a capacity problem in supply lines that were never sized for this level of simultaneous demand.

Water Heater Capacity: The Recurring Complaint

Running out of hot water is probably the single most common complaint in a converted near-campus rental, and it's rarely a mystery once you look at the numbers. A tank sized for a family of four wasn't built to supply six or eight unrelated tenants all showering within a similar morning window before classes. This is a sizing problem, not necessarily a broken water heater, and it's worth understanding that distinction before assuming a unit needs repair when it may simply need to be replaced with something sized for actual current occupancy.

What A Prior Owner's Shortcuts Can Leave Behind

Some near-campus rental conversions were done properly, with permits and inspections reflecting the change in occupancy and use. Others, particularly older conversions done decades ago under less scrutiny, sometimes weren't, and that history can leave behind plumbing modifications that technically function but don't fully meet current code, a second kitchen or bathroom added without proper permitting, for instance, or a water heater undersized for the number of bedrooms actually being rented.

Fixture count and drain sizing for multi-unit or higher-occupancy properties fall under specific requirements in the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the model code Montgomery County municipalities including Collegeville generally follow, that don't apply the same way to single-family use. A conversion done without accounting for these requirements is exactly the kind of shortcut that surfaces later as a recurring capacity complaint.

This is exactly the kind of thing worth having checked specifically on a near-campus rental, whether you're a tenant dealing with recurring issues or a landlord or property manager responsible for the building, since a shortcut from a prior owner is now your problem to identify and address, not theirs.

What Landlords And Property Managers Should Watch For

A rental property that's changed hands multiple times since its original conversion often has an incomplete or unclear history of what modifications were actually made and when. A thorough plumbing assessment when acquiring or taking over management of a near-campus rental is worth the cost specifically because it surfaces problems before they become tenant complaints or, more seriously, actual code compliance issues discovered during a future sale or inspection.

What Tenants Should Know

If you're renting near Ursinus and experiencing water pressure problems or running out of hot water regularly, this article's explanation, a mismatch between original plumbing capacity and current occupancy, is worth raising with your landlord specifically, since it points toward a real fix, sizing the water heater or supply capacity to actual current demand, rather than just another repair call that addresses symptoms without the underlying cause.

What To Check Before You Sign A Lease

Ask how many people the unit is actually rented to

More occupants than the plumbing was designed for is the root cause discussed throughout this article.

Run the shower during a likely peak time

Testing water pressure and temperature during a realistic busy period reveals more than a quiet-hour test.

Ask about the water heater's age and size

A landlord should know this, and an evasive answer is itself useful information.

Ask about any known permit history

A landlord aware of past unpermitted work should disclose it, and reluctance to answer is a real signal.

Our Approach To These Properties

Our Collegeville service page covers our broader approach to the town's wide range of housing ages and types, and near-campus rental conversions are a specific, recurring version of the age-and-history question we ask on every Collegeville call: not just how old is this house, but what has actually been done to it since it was built, and does the plumbing match what it's currently being asked to do.

We also flag genuine safety and compliance concerns honestly when we find them during a service call, rather than quietly working around an undersized system without mentioning it. An undersized water heater or an improperly permitted second bathroom is worth knowing about whether you're the tenant living with the daily inconvenience or the landlord ultimately responsible for the property's condition and any future liability tied to unpermitted work.

The Bottom Line

Near-campus Collegeville rentals carry a real, specific plumbing history beyond just their build year: a conversion from single-family use to higher-occupancy rental that often outpaced the underlying plumbing capacity. Recognizing that pattern, rather than treating every hot water or pressure complaint as an isolated repair issue, points toward the actual fix rather than another temporary patch.
