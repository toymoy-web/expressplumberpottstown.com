Why We Ask The Age Of Your Home First In Collegeville

Collegeville, originally called Freeland before its 1896 incorporation, has one of the widest housing-age ranges of any town we serve. Colonials, cottages, and Cape Cods with stone or brick exteriors from as far back as 1850 sit next to ranch-style and newer New Traditional homes built decades later. That range is exactly why the first question on a Collegeville call is usually how old the house is, since the answer changes what we expect to find.

The Perkiomen Creek forms Collegeville's western border, and homes closest to it see more groundwater pressure against older foundations than homes further into town, similar to what we see near the Schuylkill in Pottstown itself.

What We See Most In Collegeville

On the older stone and brick homes near downtown, drain and supply line age is the most common issue. Closer to the Perkiomen Creek, basement moisture and sump pump calls are more frequent, regardless of the home's age.

Collegeville's Main Street crosses the creek via the Perkiomen Bridge, a stone arch structure built in 1799 and one of the oldest of its kind still in use. Homes built in that same era near the historic crossing tend to share the original stone-and-brick construction the bridge itself is known for, while the ranch-style and newer New Traditional homes further from downtown were built to a completely different plumbing standard decades later.

Same Code, Different Starting Point

Every Collegeville plumbing job, regardless of the home's age, falls under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Montgomery County municipalities follow. The difference isn't the standard we're working to, it's how far a given house's existing plumbing already is from it, which is exactly why we check the home's actual age and materials before quoting anything.

The commute corridor along Route 422 has brought steady new construction to Collegeville over the past two decades, and those newer homes rarely need the same kind of pipe-age diagnosis an 1850s property does. We still walk through the same basic questions on every call, since assuming a newer-looking street means newer plumbing has been wrong often enough that we don't skip the check.

Collegeville is also home to Ursinus College, and a fair number of the homes and rental properties near campus were converted from single-family use decades ago, sometimes with plumbing modifications that weren't done to code at the time. We check for that specifically on any near-campus rental call, since a prior owner's shortcut is now our problem to identify.
