Why This Matters More In Pottstown

Pottstown's high water table and proximity to the Schuylkill River mean a working sump pump is doing real work here, not just sitting as backup insurance. A pump that fails during the same storm that raises the river is the scenario we get called out for most, and it's also the scenario most likely to knock out power right when the pump needs it most.

A basement that's never flooded doesn't necessarily mean the risk is low, it may just mean the pump has never actually been tested by a serious storm. We check pump age and condition even on basements with a clean history, since a first flood is exactly the kind of event a homeowner wants to prevent rather than respond to.

Common Failure Points

A stuck or worn float switch is the single most common reason a sump pump fails to activate. A clogged intake, a check valve installed backward, or a motor that's simply reached the end of its service life are the next most frequent causes. We check all of these, not just swap the unit, when a pump has failed.

A pump that runs constantly, rather than cycling on and off with actual water intake, is often a sign of a stuck switch or a check valve letting water flow back into the pit, both of which shorten the motor's remaining life if left alone.

Battery Backup: Worth Considering

A battery backup sump pump keeps working through a power outage, which matters specifically because the storms severe enough to strain your primary pump are often the same storms that knock out utility power. For a basement that's flooded before, this is one of the higher-value upgrades we install.

A battery backup unit typically runs for several hours to a day under continuous use, depending on the battery size and how often the pump is cycling, which is usually enough to cover a storm even if utility power stays out overnight. We size the battery to your pump's actual draw rather than assume a generic runtime applies to every setup.

Combination Systems And Water-Powered Backups

Some homes pair a primary electric pump with a water-powered backup that runs off municipal water pressure instead of a battery, which avoids the battery-maintenance question entirely but depends on reliable water pressure during the storm. We'll walk through which backup approach actually fits your home's setup rather than default to one option for everyone.

What To Do If Your Sump Pump Just Failed

Check the float switch first

A stuck float is the most common cause and sometimes an easy fix over the phone.

Check the breaker

A tripped breaker is a quick check before assuming the pump itself has failed.

Move items off the basement floor

Get anything on the floor up onto shelving while water rises.

Don't stand in water near the pump's outlet

Electrical safety first, especially around a submersible pump's power connection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you install battery backup systems?

Yes, and we specifically recommend them for Pottstown-area basements given the flood and power-outage risk overlap.

How often should a sump pump be serviced?

An annual check of the float switch, check valve, and discharge line catches most failures before they become emergencies.

My basement is already flooding. What do I do first?

Move valuables up, stay clear of standing water near outlets, and call. If the water is already significant, this covers the emergency extraction side of what happens next.

Can I test my sump pump myself?

Yes, pouring a bucket of water into the pit to see if the pump activates and drains properly is a simple, safe way to check it periodically.

How long do sump pumps usually last?

Most last 7 to 10 years under normal use; more with regular maintenance, less if it's been running constantly due to an underlying drainage issue.
