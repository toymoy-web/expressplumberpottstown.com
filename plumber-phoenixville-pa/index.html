An Iron Town's Housing Stock

Phoenixville grew up around the Phoenix Iron Company, and its downtown historic district, nearly 3,000 acres with over 900 contributing buildings, still shows that industrial-era housing mix: worker housing, higher-style homes built for industrial-era owners, and Victorian-era construction in Colonial, Greek Revival, Italianate, and Second Empire styles along and near Bridge Street.

Homes inside the historic district often have original cast iron or galvanized drain lines that have never been replaced, and any exterior plumbing work visible from the street can require review by the borough's Historical Architectural Review Board. We flag that early rather than after a job is scheduled.

What We See Most In Phoenixville

Drain line issues from decades-old cast iron are the most common repair call in the historic downtown core. Closer to the river and French Creek, we also see more sump pump and basement moisture calls than in Phoenixville's newer outlying construction, and we schedule those with the same priority as an emergency repair given how quickly basement water can spread.

Bridge Street's revival, including the redevelopment of the old Phoenixville Foundry site and newer riverfront apartment construction, means we also see a growing share of modern PEX and copper installations downtown alongside the historic cast iron, sometimes in the same building where a ground-floor commercial space has been updated but the residential units above haven't. The Colonial Theatre, a few blocks off Bridge Street and known nationally for its role in the 1958 film The Blob, sits roughly at the boundary between the two.

Working Within The Historic District's Rules

Plumbing work in Phoenixville still falls under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the same as the rest of Chester County, but any exterior work visible from a public street inside the historic district needs separate review by the borough's Historical Architectural Review Board. We identify that requirement before scheduling the work, not after a crew shows up, and we'll walk you through any added cost or timeline for the review before we schedule the repair.

Outside the historic core, Phoenixville's newer riverfront development and outlying neighborhoods don't carry the same review requirement, which is one more reason we ask for the address upfront rather than assuming every Phoenixville call needs the same paperwork. A Bridge Street loft and a newer home a mile from downtown are, for our purposes, two different jobs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you work on homes in Phoenixville's historic downtown district?

Yes, and we account for the borough's Historical Architectural Review Board process on any visible exterior work.

Is Phoenixville really not in Montgomery County?

Correct, Phoenixville is in Chester County, even though it's close to and often grouped with Pottstown and other Montgomery County towns.

Do you work on cast iron drain lines?

Yes, cast iron is common in Phoenixville's older downtown homes, and we service and replace sections of it as needed.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Phoenixville?

Yes, we cover both of Phoenixville's ZIP codes, the borough's own 19460 and the neighboring 19453 that reaches into Mont Clare, from the Bridge Street downtown core out toward the borough's outlying edges.
