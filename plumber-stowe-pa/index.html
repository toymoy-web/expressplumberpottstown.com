Where Pottstown's Main Street Keeps Going

West Pottsgrove, the township Stowe sits inside, is the smallest of the municipalities touching Pottstown's border, small enough that a transit rider can cross the entire community on the Pottstown Area Rapid Transit Green Line in a few minutes flat. Roughly 3,600 people call Stowe home, a figure that's held steady for decades rather than growing or shrinking much.

The Pottstown Expressway, Route 422, also cuts through the township, giving Stowe direct highway access back toward Pottstown regardless of which part of the community you're calling from.

The Pottstown Expressway, Route 422, also runs through West Pottsgrove Township, giving Stowe fast, direct access back to Pottstown regardless of which part of the community you're in.

West Pottsgrove Township itself is the smallest of the townships directly bordering Pottstown, but that compact size works in Stowe's favor for service purposes, since nothing in the community sits more than a few minutes from our Pottstown base regardless of traffic conditions on Route 422 at any given time of day.

What We See Most In Stowe

West Pottsgrove Township's housing mix runs close to Pottstown's own mid-20th-century character in the sections nearest the borough line, with somewhat newer construction further into the township. The plumbing issues we see here track closely with what we see in Pottstown itself: aging supply lines, water heater sediment from the area's moderately hard water, and the occasional drain line showing its age.

Because Stowe sits right where Pottstown's own High Street corridor continues into the township, a number of the homes here were built in roughly the same era and by many of the same builders as the adjacent Pottstown blocks, which is part of why the plumbing issues we see track so closely between the two.
