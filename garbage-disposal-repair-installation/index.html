Humming But Not Spinning? Usually A Jam

A disposal that hums but doesn't turn almost always has something jammed against the impeller plate, often something as simple as a fruit pit or a piece of silverware that fell in. Most units have a manual reset button on the underside and an Allen-wrench port for manually freeing a jammed impeller, both of which we check before assuming the motor itself has failed.

A disposal that trips its reset button repeatedly after being cleared usually points to a motor working harder than it should, either from an ongoing jam we haven't fully cleared or a motor genuinely nearing the end of its service life.

Leaking From The Bottom: Usually The End Of The Line

A disposal leaking from the bottom housing, rather than from the sink flange or the connections to the dishwasher and drain, typically means the internal seal has failed. Unlike a top-side leak, which is often a simple connection fix, a bottom-housing leak generally means the unit needs replacement rather than repair.

Disposal installation and drain connections fall under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which specifies trap and venting requirements a disposal's drain line still has to meet even though the unit itself is a small appliance, not a major fixture.

Choosing A Replacement Unit

Horsepower, noise insulation, and grinding chamber size are the real differences between disposal tiers. A household that runs the disposal daily with fibrous scraps benefits from a higher horsepower unit; a household with light, occasional use doesn't need to pay for capacity it won't use. We'll size the replacement to how you actually use it, not just sell the top tier.

A disposal that's original to a mid-century Pottstown kitchen renovation, or the same era of housing common in nearby Limerick, is often long past its realistic service life even if it still technically runs, and replacing it proactively is usually cheaper than waiting for a failure that also damages the connected drain line.

Noise insulation has improved substantially in newer units, so a household bothered by an older, loud disposal often finds a mid-tier modern replacement noticeably quieter even without upgrading to the top horsepower option.

We also confirm the dishwasher air-gap or drain connection is routed correctly during any replacement, since a miswired connection here is a surprisingly common source of dishwasher backflow complaints that get mistakenly blamed on the dishwasher itself.

Getting this connection right the first time avoids a callback later, and it's a detail that's easy to overlook if the installer is only focused on the disposal unit itself.

Before We Arrive

Turn off the disposal at the switch

Never reach into a disposal, even one that seems off, without cutting power first.

Check for a tripped reset button

The small red button on the unit's underside sometimes resolves a jam on its own once pressed.

Note any unusual smell

A persistent odor even after running the disposal can indicate trapped debris worth mentioning.

Don't use chemical drain cleaners

These can damage a disposal's internal components; avoid them if the unit is also slow to drain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to reach into a jammed disposal?

Only after the unit is unplugged or switched off at the breaker. Most jams can be cleared with the included Allen wrench from the top instead.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Most last 8 to 12 years with normal use; less if it regularly grinds fibrous scraps like celery or corn husks that strain the motor.

Can you install a disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, assuming the sink has the right drain configuration or can be adapted, and there's accessible power nearby.

What shouldn't go down a garbage disposal?

Fibrous items like celery, corn husks, and onion skins, along with grease, bones, and coffee grounds, all shorten a disposal's working life.

Do all disposal brands use the same mounting system?

Most use a similar universal mounting collar, though we confirm compatibility with your existing sink setup before ordering a replacement.
