Common Installation Projects We Handle

Replacing an aging fiberglass tub or shower surround, converting a tub to a walk-in shower for easier access, and upgrading an old two-handle valve to a modern single-handle pressure-balancing valve are the most common projects we see. Each involves more than just the visible fixture, the plumbing behind the wall, the waterproofing membrane, and the drain connection all have to be done correctly for the finished result to actually last.

A fiberglass surround original to a mid-century Pottstown bathroom, or the similar-era housing common in Limerick, has often outlasted its intended lifespan by a decade or more, and cracks or chalky discoloration are usually the first visible sign that the surround's waterproofing integrity, not just its appearance, is starting to fail.

Why Waterproofing Matters More Than The Fixture Itself

A beautiful new shower installed over inadequate waterproofing will leak into the wall cavity or the floor below within a few years, causing damage that costs far more than the original installation. We treat the waterproofing membrane and proper slope to the drain as the real foundation of the job, not an afterthought behind the visible tile or surround.

Fixture and drain installation for any shower or tub replacement falls under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which specifies requirements for trap configuration, venting, and slope that a rushed installation can easily miss even when the visible tile work looks perfect.

Valve Upgrades: Often Worth Doing At The Same Time

An old two-handle valve is more prone to leaks and doesn't offer the scald protection a modern pressure-balancing valve does, similar to the upgrade path we recommend during a faucet or fixture repair elsewhere in the house. If we're already opening the wall for a shower or tub replacement, upgrading the valve at the same time avoids paying for that same wall access twice.

Pressure-balancing and thermostatic valves are now standard in most new installations specifically because they prevent the sudden temperature spike that happens when someone flushes a toilet or puts sudden demand on the water heater while you're in the shower, a real safety consideration in households with young children or older adults.

Replacing a valve without replacing the surrounding tile or surround is possible in many cases, since the valve body itself is usually accessible from behind the wall without disturbing the finished surface in front, depending on the specific installation.

An access panel installed on the opposite side of the wall, in a closet or hallway, is sometimes added specifically to make future valve service possible without cutting into finished tile at all, worth considering during a new installation even if it adds a small upfront cost.

We'll flag this option specifically during any new valve installation discussion, since it's a small addition at install time that saves real hassle on any future service call.

What To Check Before You Call

Note your current fixture type

Tell us if you have a tub, a fiberglass surround, or a tiled shower already.

Measure the rough space if possible

A rough width and depth helps us prepare options before the first visit.

Think about accessibility needs

A walk-in conversion is worth discussing if long-term mobility is a consideration.

Note any existing leaks or soft flooring

Signs of past water damage around the fixture change the project's scope.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you convert my tub to a walk-in shower?

Yes, this is one of our most common installation projects, particularly for homeowners wanting easier long-term access.

Should I upgrade my valve while you're already in the wall?

It's usually worth considering, since it avoids paying for wall access separately later if the old valve fails.

How long does a typical installation take?

A straightforward fixture replacement can be a day or two; a full tub-to-shower conversion with tile work takes longer depending on scope.

Do you handle the tile work too?

We handle the plumbing, waterproofing membrane, and fixture installation; tile selection and installation can be coordinated as part of the project or handled separately.

Is a walk-in shower conversion a good resale investment?

It often is, particularly in homes marketed toward buyers wanting single-floor accessibility, though the value depends on your local market.
