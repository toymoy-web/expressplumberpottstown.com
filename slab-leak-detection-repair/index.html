Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Pottstown, PA

A warm spot on the floor, water coming up under floorboards, or an unexplained spike in your water bill can mean a leak under your home's slab. Finding it precisely is what keeps the fix small.

Why Slab Leaks Happen

Copper supply lines run under a concrete slab can corrode or shift slightly over decades, eventually developing a pinhole leak. Because the line is under a home's foundation, the leak isn't visible until water finds its way up through a crack or along the edge of the slab, often near a garage or an exterior wall.

Pottstown's moderate water hardness plays a role here too, mineral content in the water gradually contributes to pipe wall wear on aging copper, the same underlying issue that shortens water heater lifespan across our service area.

Slab leaks tend to cluster by neighborhood and construction era, since homes built during the same period with the same copper supplier and installation crew often develop similar issues around the same age, which is part of why we ask about a home's build year early in the diagnosis.

How We Confirm It's A Slab Leak Specifically

We use acoustic detection equipment to trace the sound of pressurized water through the slab itself, then confirm the location with thermal imaging before cutting into any concrete. This precision, following the same diagnosis-first approach used in our broader leak detection service, is what keeps a slab leak repair to a small, targeted opening instead of tearing up an entire section of floor.

Repair Options

A direct spot repair, accessing just the leak location, works when the surrounding pipe is otherwise sound. Rerouting the line above the slab avoids cutting concrete entirely in some cases. A full repipe is worth discussing if the home has copper of an age where multiple slab leaks are likely over time, not just this one. Any repair method follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) requirements for pipe support and material once the line is exposed.

Once the leak location is confirmed, we also check surrounding sections of exposed pipe for early signs of the same corrosion pattern, since a slab leak on one section of aging copper is a reasonable signal the rest of the system is approaching the same age-related risk.

That extra check costs nothing extra and can catch a second developing problem before it becomes its own urgent, separate repair call down the road.

What To Check Before You Call

Note where the warm or wet spot is

Its location relative to the water heater and main lines helps narrow down the search.

Check your water meter

Shut off every fixture and see if the meter is still moving, confirming an active leak.

Don't cut into the slab yourself

Guessing at the location risks unnecessary concrete damage; let detection narrow it down first.

Watch your water bill

A sudden, unexplained increase is often the first sign before any physical symptom appears.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if it's a slab leak and not something else?

A warm spot on the floor, water at the edge of the slab, or a water bill spike with no visible cause are the classic signs. We confirm with acoustic and thermal detection before any repair.

Will you need to break up my floor?

Only at the confirmed leak location, and only after detection narrows it down precisely. We don't open concrete on a guess.

Is a slab leak covered by insurance?

Often the resulting water damage is covered, though the leak repair itself may not be. Check your specific policy.

How long does a slab leak repair take?

A confirmed spot repair is often completed in a day; rerouting or a full repipe takes longer depending on scope.

Can a slab leak cause structural damage to my home?

Over time, yes, if left unaddressed, ongoing moisture under a foundation can affect soil stability and the slab itself, which is part of why early detection and prompt repair genuinely matter here.
