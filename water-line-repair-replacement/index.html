How This Differs From An In-Home Leak

Your home's water service line runs underground from the municipal main or well to your house, separate from the interior supply lines leak detection and repiping typically address. A break in this line often shows up as a soggy patch of yard, an unexplained water bill spike, or reduced pressure throughout the entire house rather than at just one fixture.

The service line is also the one pipe on the property that typically predates the house itself changing hands multiple times, since it's rarely touched during interior renovations. That means a service line's actual age and material are sometimes a genuine unknown until we excavate to check it directly.

Signs Your Service Line Has A Problem

A consistently soft or soggy area in the yard along the presumed line path, especially one that doesn't dry out after several days without rain, is one of the clearest signs. Low water pressure across every fixture in the house, not just one, points here rather than to an internal leak. Water pooling near the street-side shutoff or meter pit is another common sign.

An unusually green or fast-growing patch of grass along the line's path, even in a dry stretch of weather, is a subtler sign worth mentioning, since a small underground leak can act like a hidden irrigation line for whatever's growing directly above it.

Repair Vs. Full Line Replacement

A localized break can often be repaired with a spot excavation once we've confirmed the exact location. A line that's failed due to age, material, or a break in a spot that suggests broader deterioration, common in original supply lines matching Pottstown's mid-20th-century housing stock, is a better candidate for full replacement. Trenchless replacement is an option for many service line jobs, minimizing yard disruption compared to full-length excavation.

Service line replacement work falls under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and depending on the scope, may require coordination with Pottstown Borough's water authority at the connection point, similar to the permit process for a sewer lateral replacement.

What A Failing Service Line Looks Like Over Time

A slow decline in water pressure over months, rather than a sudden drop, often means a service line is gradually failing rather than experiencing one clean break. Discolored water that clears after running for a minute, especially after periods of no water use, can indicate corrosion inside an aging service line rather than anything happening inside the house itself.

What To Note Before You Call

Locate the soggy or unusual spot

Note roughly where in the yard it is relative to the house and street.

Check your water meter

Shut off every fixture and see if the meter is still running, confirming an active leak.

Know your main shutoff location

Useful if the leak is severe enough to need the water off entirely.

Check recent water bills

A gradual or sudden increase helps us understand how long this has likely been happening.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from a leak inside my house?

A service line leak happens underground between the street and your house, before the water ever enters the home, and needs excavation rather than an indoor repair.

Can a service line leak affect my water pressure everywhere?

Yes, a significant leak upstream of the house typically reduces pressure at every fixture, not just one area.

Will my whole yard need to be dug up?

Not necessarily. Trenchless methods and spot excavation both minimize yard disruption compared to full-length trenching, depending on the specific damage.

Who's responsible for the line, me or the utility?

Typically the homeowner is responsible for the line from the curb stop to the house; the utility maintains the main and the connection at the street. We'll clarify your specific situation on-site.

How long does a service line replacement take?

Trenchless jobs are often completed in a day; traditional excavation can take longer depending on length and site conditions.
