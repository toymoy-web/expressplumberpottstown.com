A Township Built In Two Very Different Eras

William Evans's family arrived from Limerick, Ireland in 1698, and the township formally took shape in 1726. Landmarks from that early period, including the 1763 William and Mordecai Evans House, remain standing today, even as the surrounding population climbed from roughly 6,600 residents in 1990 to somewhere between 18,000 and 20,000 now. Because of that surge, a single day of service calls here might involve a stone farmhouse on one street and a subdivision built in the 2000s a few blocks over.

The practical difference matters. An older Limerick farmhouse is more likely to have original galvanized supply lines and a well or septic system to account for, while a newer subdivision home is more likely to have PEX plumbing and a public water tap, with problems that run more toward fixture wear than pipe age.

What We See Most In Limerick

Water heater issues lead our Limerick calls, closely followed by drain clogs in the older sections of the township near the historic core. Newer construction tends to call more for fixture and faucet repairs than for the pipe-age problems older homes see.

The township is served by the Spring-Ford Area School District, and many of the newer subdivisions built during Limerick's growth phase sit within a few miles of that district's schools. The Isaac Hunsberger House, a National Register of Historic Places property near Ridge Pike, marks roughly where the older, colonial-era section of the township gives way to newer development, a useful rule of thumb for guessing a home's likely plumbing age before we even arrive.

Installation Standards Don't Change With The House's Age

Whether we're working on an original farmhouse line or a 2005 subdivision build, plumbing work in Limerick Township falls under the same International Plumbing Code (IPC) framework Montgomery County municipalities generally follow. An older property is more likely to need updates to bring existing work up to that standard, which we'll flag honestly rather than assume compliance either way.

We keep a mental map of which parts of the township are older colonial-era sections versus which subdivisions went in during the 1990s and 2000s growth boom, since that context changes what we bring on the truck before we even see the house. A tech who shows up expecting PEX and finds galvanized supply lines instead loses time; we'd rather ask the right questions on the phone first.
